# Voice

Read this before writing a word. The facts come from Jira. The meaning comes from the person who shipped it. The voice is what makes the result read like Oolio rather than like a changelog.

## The shape of it

Craft plus candour. Restraint, the object described plainly, and the honest shape of the capability curve where the product is AI.

For Ngara this is explicit: still at school, learning fast, will sometimes be wrong, and the least capable it will ever be. For everything else, keep the restraint and drop the curve. A menu builder does not need a growth narrative.

## Rules

- **No em dashes.** Commas, colons, brackets, a full stop. Not in a body, not in a title, not in a slug.
- **Short declarative sentences.** One idea per sentence. Australian English spelling, which is the house British English: colour, organise, centre.
- **Second person for the customer, first person plural for Oolio.** "You can now group by venue." "We made the reports you have change level."
- **Titles are a claim, not a label.** "One menu. Every device." "The same number, everywhere you meet it." "Give more people the numbers." "It has been paying attention." Never "Release notes 28 September" as a headline.
- **Lead with why. Never a ticket list.** The what is ordered by how much it hurt, not by issue type.
- **Demote the rest.** Fixes get one line each. No reproduction steps, no ticket keys, no internal names on anything customer facing.
- **State what is not covered.** "Back Office only for now." "Five of the six permission groups." The honesty buys credibility for everything else on the page.
- **No engineering vanity metrics.** No commit, PR or ticket counts in a business update.
- **No Jira keys, internal team names or customer names on the customer page.** Keys and customers belong on the Confluence page and in the Teams message, where the audience is internal.
- **A fix-only release is written as confidence, not apology.** It has a reason it mattered.
- **At most one recognisably borrowed line per release**, flagged on handover. "The least capable it will ever be" is Altman's, not ours.
- **Vary the shape.** If every release opens with a confession, the confession stops meaning anything.

## The four registers

Same facts, four lengths, four readers. Never a copy and paste of each other.

| Artefact | Reader | Length | Carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teams message | Product, sales, AM, support, onboarding | 300 to 600 words | The why, every theme, the incidents that came with it, what is not shipped, the next two releases, the link out |
| Customer page (HubSpot) | Customers and partners | 400 to 700 words | The why in the customer's terms, the changes as outcomes, what is not covered, the next two releases, a help centre CTA |
| Confluence page | The product team and anyone auditing later | As long as the facts need | The record: version, date, every item with its key and status, the narrative, links to the other three |
| Slack message | Whoever lives in Slack | 6 to 10 lines | One line of why, three bullets at most, the link |

## Words

Banned by default, from the house style: leverage, utilise, seamless, robust, empower, streamline, synergy, best-in-class, cutting-edge, holistic. Also: "enhancements", "improvements" as a heading, "we are excited", "we are pleased to announce", emoji in headings, "WIP" on anything a customer can see.

Prefer: the verb the operator would use. Group, pin, export, schedule, connect, send.

## The test

Read the headline aloud. If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it. If it sounds like someone who ran the venue and then built the product, it is right.
